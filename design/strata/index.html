Identity and least privilege for agents: strong demand, almost no research

  1. 1

    Platform vendors have shipped non-human-identity products for agents, and enterprises rate scoped credentials for AI among their top needs. 3.5 mean, 18 of 28 at 4+

    MicrosoftAWSPalantir
  2. 2

    The inventory's coverage of infra.identity and agent.authz is: 20 papers

  3. 3

    What exists treats authorisation as a design constraint on the agent loop, or tests whether a model will obey an operator rule at all - neither is an authorisation mechanism.

    Design patternsRuLES
  4. 4

    Gap: the delegation problem - how an agent proves which principal it acts for, and how a downstream service bounds that - is being solved in product without a research literature to draw on.

Closes when
Peer-reviewed work defines how an agent proves which principal it acts for across a tool call, and how a downstream service bounds that delegation - with an implementation and a threat model covering a hijacked agent.
Blocked by
Vendors are shipping proprietary answers (Entra Agent ID, scoped tokens) faster than anyone is publishing a general model, so there is no shared vocabulary to evaluate them against.
Falsifier
A venue paper, or an adopted standard, specifying agent-to-service delegation semantics.
Against it
This may be an artefact of where the work is published rather than whether it exists. Identity and authorisation are classical systems-security problems with mature literature (OAuth, capabilities, macaroons); practitioners may simply be applying it without writing AI-specific papers. The gap is then in *translation*, not in knowledge.

AgentDojo: A Dynamic Environment to Evaluate Prompt Injection Attacks and Defenses for LLM Agents

Edoardo Debenedetti, Jie Zhang, Mislav Balunović, Luca Beurer-Kellner and 2 more. ETH Zurich, Invariant Labs.

Security benchmarksAgent hijackingPrompt & indirect injectionContext provenance & spotlighting

What it is about

The reference benchmark for agent prompt injection: 97 realistic tasks and 629 security cases where utility and attack success are measured together. Its key finding is that published defences all degrade the agent's usefulness without closing the hole.

Threat model

An agent with real tools (email, banking, travel) exposed to attacker-controlled tool output.

What it leaves open

Exposes that every tested defence trades utility for security, and no defence holds; the frontier it marks - a defence that is both - is still open.
